# Seemon Kumawat

Spatial scientist in Darwin, Northern Territory. Master of Applied Spatial
Science (Geographic Information Science, Cartography and Remote Sensing),
Charles Darwin University.

As a research assistant at CDU I reviewed published studies on how well
different platforms detect gamba grass and buffel grass, two invasive grasses
that threaten northern Australian savannas: UAV, LiDAR, Sentinel-2, Landsat and
airborne imagery, weighed against each other on spatial resolution, revisit
frequency and cost. These grasses change fuel loads and fire behaviour across
the Top End, so how accurately they can be mapped shapes how the country is
managed.

I also worked on GIS projects, digitising and annotating datasets for invasive
species mapping and ghost net detection, and preparing analysis-ready data in
ArcGIS Pro and QGIS.

## Work

- Classified Sentinel-2 imagery in Google Earth Engine with supervised (CART)
  and unsupervised (k-means) methods, and assessed accuracy with a confusion
  matrix, overall accuracy and kappa
- Modelled bushfire-prone zones for the Darwin rural area from fire frequency
  rasters and land use data
- Mapped urban heat exposure across Darwin through weighted overlay in
  ArcGIS Pro
- Delineated flood-prone areas in Jhapa District, Nepal from DEM and
  hydrological layers
- Ran a network analysis of travel times to hospitals across Darwin and
  Palmerston, and produced service area maps

## Tools

Remote sensing and GIS: ArcGIS Pro, QGIS, Google Earth Engine, Sentinel-2
Methods: image classification, accuracy assessment, weighted overlay, terrain
and hydrological analysis, network analysis, thematic cartography,
digitisation and annotation
Statistics: Stata, R

## Interests

Environmental monitoring and natural resource management, Earth observation,
savanna fire regimes, invasive species mapping, and work grounded in northern
Australian landscapes.
